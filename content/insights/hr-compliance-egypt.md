For more than two decades, private-sector employment in Egypt was governed by Labour Law No. 12 of 2003. That changed in 2025. [Labour Law No. 14 of 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-enacts-new-labor-law-with-changes-affecting-employers-beginning-1-september-2025) was issued on 3 May 2025, replaced the 2003 law and took effect on 1 September 2025, with the new labour courts handling disputes from 1 October 2025.

Large employers with in-house legal teams moved quickly. Many SMEs did not, often because their HR files were already informal and the change felt abstract. More than a year on, the details are settling, and implementing decisions from the Ministry of Labour continue to appear. This is a good moment to check that your contracts, internal regulations and HR records match the law as it now stands.

This article summarizes the changes most relevant to small and mid-sized employers and sets out a practical review. It is not legal advice. The executive decisions issued under the law can change details, so confirm specific points with a qualified labour lawyer before acting.

## Contracts and records

**Written contracts in four copies.** Employment contracts are now executed in [four counterparts](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-enacts-new-labor-law-with-changes-affecting-employers-beginning-1-september-2025): for the employer, the employee, the social insurance office and the competent administrative authority. Contracts are in Arabic, and non-Arabic-speaking foreign employees may also have a version in their own language.

**Fixed term only where the work requires it.** Under the law, a contract may be concluded for a fixed period [if the nature of the work requires it](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Translation-of-Labor-law-No.-14-of-2025.pdf) (Article 87). A contract is treated as indefinite if it is not written, does not specify a duration, or if both parties continue working after a fixed term ends without a written renewal (Article 88). Many SMEs still roll over fixed-term contracts by habit; this is the point to check first.

**Probation.** Probation [may not exceed three months, and an employee may not be placed on probation more than once with the same employer](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Translation-of-Labor-law-No.-14-of-2025.pdf) (Article 90).

**Employee files.** Employers must keep each employee's file for [at least five years after the employment ends](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Translation-of-Labor-law-No.-14-of-2025.pdf) (Article 92), and until any related dispute is finally decided. Employers must also submit a statement of employee data and salaries and [update it each January](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-enacts-new-labor-law-with-changes-affecting-employers-beginning-1-september-2025).

## Pay and working time

**Annual increment.** Employers must grant a periodic increase of [at least 3% of the insured salary](https://amereller.com/publication/egypt-new-labor-law-no-14-of-2025/). Reducing or waiving it for economic reasons requires approval from the National Wages Council.

**Overtime.** Law-firm summaries report that prior authorization for overtime has been [replaced by notification to the Ministry within seven days](https://amereller.com/publication/egypt-new-labor-law-no-14-of-2025/), and that the daily limit is now framed as a cap on total time at the workplace. Check how your shift patterns and overtime records fit.

**Training fund contribution.** The old contribution based on net profits has been replaced by a per-employee contribution of [0.25% of the minimum insured salary, between EGP 10 and EGP 30](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-enacts-new-labor-law-with-changes-affecting-employers-beginning-1-september-2025). Confirm with your adviser whether and how it applies to your headcount.

## Leave

The law updated several leave entitlements. According to [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-enacts-new-labor-law-with-changes-affecting-employers-beginning-1-september-2025) and [Amereller](https://amereller.com/publication/egypt-new-labor-law-no-14-of-2025/):

- **Annual leave:** 15 working days in the first year, 21 days from the second year, 30 days after ten full years of service, and 45 days for employees with disabilities.
- **Maternity leave:** four months of paid leave, available up to three times during employment, with no minimum service requirement.
- **Paternity:** paid leave on the day of the child's birth, up to three times, not deducted from annual leave.
- **Childcare leave:** unpaid, up to two years, available up to three times, subject to service and establishment-size conditions.

If your leave policy, payroll system or leave forms still reflect the 2003 rules, they need updating.

## Flexible and remote work

The law recognizes [remote, part-time, flexible-hours and job-sharing arrangements](https://amereller.com/publication/egypt-new-labor-law-no-14-of-2025/) with the same rights and obligations as traditional employment. This is useful for SMEs competing for scarce skills, but it means hybrid arrangements should be documented in the contract or an annex, not agreed informally on a messaging app.

## Ending employment

This is where informal practices create the most risk.

- **Resignations** must be [ratified by the competent labour authority](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/egypt-enacts-new-labor-law-with-changes-affecting-employers-beginning-1-september-2025), and the employer has ten days to respond. Pre-signed resignation letters, a long-standing practice in some workplaces, should not be relied upon.
- **Notice:** major advisers report a [three-month notice period](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/services/tax/perspectives/egypt-new-labor-law-entering-into-effect.html) for terminating indefinite contracts and contracts of more than five years.
- **Compensation and grounds** for termination are set out in detail in the law. Commentaries differ on some points, so take legal advice on any specific case rather than relying on a summary.
- **Harassment and bullying** in the workplace are now [defined and criminalized](https://amereller.com/publication/egypt-new-labor-law-no-14-of-2025/), and employers who fail to act can face liability.

## Disputes and internal regulations

Specialized [labour courts](https://amereller.com/publication/egypt-new-labor-law-no-14-of-2025/) now sit within the courts of first instance and appeal, effective 1 October 2025. Faster, specialized courts make documentation more important: the employer with clear contracts, policies and records is in a far better position.

Every employer with [ten or more employees must establish internal work regulations](https://eg.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Translation-of-Labor-law-No.-14-of-2025.pdf) (Article 137). In 2026, the Minister of Labour issued [Ministerial Decree No. 162 of 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/employee-rights-labour-relations/1842704/egypts-new-model-work-regulations-are-now-in-force-what-you-need-to-know), setting out what these regulations must cover, from recruitment, pay and leave to flexible work, anti-harassment measures, grievances and discipline, and how they are filed with the Labour Directorate for certification. If your internal regulations were written under the old law, or do not exist, this is a priority.

## A practical HR compliance review

We suggest SMEs work through six steps.

1. **Inventory.** List every employee, their contract type, start date, salary, insured salary and work arrangement. Flag anyone without a signed written contract.
2. **Contracts.** Update your contract templates to the new law and any implementing decisions. Review every fixed-term contract: is the fixed term justified by the nature of the work, and has it been properly renewed in writing?
3. **Internal work regulations.** If you have ten or more employees, prepare or update regulations that cover the topics required by the 2026 decree, and file them for certification.
4. **Policies and payroll.** Align leave, overtime, flexible work, annual increment and anti-harassment policies with the law. Make sure your payroll system calculates entitlements correctly.
5. **Termination procedures.** Write down a step-by-step procedure for resignations, performance issues, disciplinary investigations and termination, including notice and documentation. Stop any reliance on pre-signed resignations.
6. **Records and calendar.** Organize employee files so they can be produced quickly, keep them for the required period, and add the January data update and other filings to your compliance calendar. Remember that employee files contain personal data and must be protected accordingly.

## Turning compliance into good management

Labour law compliance is often treated as a defensive exercise. It is also an opportunity. Clear contracts, fair and written rules, and consistent procedures reduce disputes, make managers more confident, and help SMEs attract people who have choices about where they work.

The new law has raised the baseline. Employers who use this moment to put their HR house in order will spend less time on disputes and more time on building their teams.
