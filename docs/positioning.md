# Darwish & Co. — Positioning, Services and Content (v3)

**Reconstruction of the v3 document, 16 Sep 2026.** The v3 file was lost. This rebuilds it
from the four surviving inputs: the v1 draft (15 Sep 2026), the reviewer's assessment of
v1, the reviewer's positioning, and the positioning finally agreed. It supersedes the
thinner reconstruction committed earlier today.

Where v1 and the reviewer disagreed, **the reviewer's judgement is taken as settled** —

> **A wise word in the right moment.**
>
> Darwish & Co. is an Egypt-based governance and management consultancy for SMEs in the
> MENA region. We help you build the governance, controls and growth strategy that
> investors, major clients and regulators expect.

This is live on the site today (change log Entry 1, commit `7c99416`).

> Darwish & Co. is a governance and management consultancy based in New Cairo, Egypt,
> serving SMEs across Egypt and the MENA region.

**Disambiguation** ⚠️ — for the About page and structured data, once the founder confirms
it is accurate:

> Darwish & Co. (darwish-co.com) is an Egyptian consultancy headquartered in New Cairo,
> not affiliated with similarly named Darwish companies in Qatar.

### What changed from v1, and why

| v1 proposed | v3 position | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Governance that helps growing companies move faster, not slower." | The agreed subheadline above | v1's headline promised speed without showing the mechanism |
| "Governance **and strategy** consultancy" | "Governance and management consultancy" | Keeps governance as the distinguishing focus; strategy execution is the outcome, not a co-equal claim |
| Descriptor claimed Gulf/MENA delivery | Target market stated; delivery footprint stated separately | Reviewer: "Target market and established service footprint must remain separate" |
| Five "moments" as the site's entry structure | Two priority buying situations (§3) | Five moments are five different buying processes — too many launch priorities for a small firm |
| AI governance as the spearhead | An offer to validate, not the lead | Nothing established a delivery lead, a defined engagement or buyer willingness to spend |

### Voice rules (carried over from v1, unchanged by the reviewer)

- Calm, plain, precise: advice from someone who has seen the problem before.
- Lead with the owner's situation, then what changes, then what they get.
- Never: "world-class", "leading", "comprehensive", "tailored solutions", "top-tier",
  "unlock", "bold", unsourced ratings or client counts.
- Always: who it's for, what you receive, roughly how long, and who delivers (Darwish or a
  named partner).
- Family ownership, relationships and hierarchy are design constraints, never excuses.

### Claims that were removed and must not return

- "No junior bench" and "the consultant you meet is the one who delivers" — operating
  commitments the delivery model may not be able to keep. **Permitted replacement:**
  "A named senior lead is accountable for each engagement", once confirmed.
- "A small firm can't win strategy consulting Egypt."
- "AI governance has the least competition."
- Any big-number stat tile without a published source.

## 2. Direction: governance-led, validated rather than assumed

Governance is the organising direction. It is **a position to validate, not a proven
beachhead.** The evidence supports testing it, and no more:

- In the audit's AI tests, Darwish's only non-branded appearance on both ChatGPT and
  Gemini was the GRC recommendation prompt. That is two single runs of one prompt — an
  existing association, not proof of demand, revenue or outcomes.
- Neither test assessed the AI-governance or anti-bribery niche at all.

**Expansion is earned by commercial traction, not by AI mentions.** AI visibility is an
observation channel, kept alongside the real measures: qualified enquiries, paid
engagements, delivery capacity, repeatable outcomes and references. Egypt first; the Gulf
only once delivery coverage exists.

### The four-part test every service must pass

From the reviewer, and binding. For each service: **a named delivery lead · a defined
scope · a sample output · relevant experience.** If any one is missing, it is an offer
under development and is not listed as a service.

Services flagged as exceeding what the biographies alone justify, and therefore requiring
the test before publication: family constitutions and dividend policies · board design and
succession · whistleblowing and investigation procedures · certification support · AI
procurement and oversight · PMO set-up.

### AI Governance — conditions

Listed as a service only with a named, available delivery lead and clear responsibility for
technical, organisational and jurisdiction-specific questions. The defensible starting
offer is narrower than "AI Governance": ⚠️ **an AI-use governance review** covering an
inventory of AI uses, responsibilities, acceptable-use rules and human-review requirements.

"Six Controls for Human Oversight" is explained **in relation to** established guidance —
ISO/IEC 42001 addresses a broader AI management system — and never presented as a complete
governance system of its own.

### ISO 37001 — required precision

Readiness, implementation assistance and independent certification are three different
things and must be distinguished on the page. **ISO develops the standard; it does not
certify organisations.** The current edition is ISO 37001:2025.

### Partner-delivered work

VisionEthics (anti-bribery, ISO 37001) and In Light (research) are named on the service
page itself, stating what the partner delivers and who is accountable. Their expertise is
attributed to them, never absorbed into Darwish's own offer.

### Named methods

v1 proposed Decision Rights Framework, Integrity Programme in 90 Days, Six Controls for
Human Oversight, Plan-to-Pulse. **v3 holds these back.** Naming a process does not make it
proprietary, validated or effective, and "Integrity Programme in 90 Days" promises a
delivery timeline before scope and capacity are confirmed. Start with a clear process and
genuine work products; name a method when it has the substance to deserve one.

### Service page anatomy

H1 and a one-line promise · "Is this you?" (3–5 situations in the owner's words) · How we
help, each component with its deliverable · Our process, with typical duration per stage ·
Standards genuinely applied · Who leads this (named, with partner accountability where
relevant) · FAQ · link to the hub and related spokes · one specific call to action.

**"Examples of our work" may contain only consented, real cases.** An illustrative or
teaching scenario is labelled and presented separately — never under that heading.

## 5. Proof, in order

The reviewer's order, which corrects v1's. Each step outranks the one below it:

1. **Verified people** — one page per consultant: credentials, LinkedIn, what they wrote or reviewed.
2. **Actual work products** — real deliverables, redacted as needed.
3. **Consented case studies** — problem → what we did → result.
4. **Specific partner corroboration** — the partner confirming its role, in its own words.
5. **Original research** — later, and smaller than v1 proposed: ⚠️ a clearly described
   interview study rather than a "100+ respondent survey", which is not automatically
   representative. A governance statistic carries a date and is not treated as timeless.

Named frameworks and scored diagnostics rank **below** demonstrated work, not above it.

Published integrity commitments (`/about/integrity`) stay in the plan: an anti-corruption
consultancy that publishes its own conflict-of-interest and confidentiality rules is
consistent with its claim.

## 6. Content: one hub, four spokes, and a separate engagement page

v1 proposed six pillars and 39 spokes — 45 articles, roughly 20 months of production at two
per month. v3 replaces it with **one hub and four spokes**, plus one standalone site page.

### The hub

⚠️ **Governance for Growing SMEs: A Practical Guide for Owners in Egypt and the MENA Region**

Defines the position, introduces the topic and links to all four spokes and the related
services.

> **Open decision:** the reviewer's version read "Owner-Led and Family Businesses in
> Egypt". The agreed positioning says "SMEs in the MENA region". The title above follows
> the agreed positioning while keeping Egypt visible. Confirm which wins.

### The four spokes

Titles optimised in v3 to tie each spoke back to the governance hub rather than reading as
a separate topic. ⚠️ Reconstructed wording — the intent is certain, the exact phrasing is not.

| # | Spoke | Optimised from | Related service |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Delegation of Authority: How to Delegate Decisions Without Losing Oversight** | "How to Delegate Decisions Without Losing Oversight" | Corporate & Family Governance |
| 2 | **What to Do When a Major Client Sends an Anti-Bribery Questionnaire** | "How to Respond to a Major Client's Anti-Bribery Questionnaire" | Risk, Compliance & Integrity |
| 3 | **How to Turn Strategy into Decision Rights, Measures and Monthly Reviews** | "How to Turn Growth Strategy into Clear Responsibilities, KPIs and Action" | Strategy & Execution |
| 4 | **Setting the Rules for Employee AI Use: A Governance Starting Point** | "What Rules Should a Company Set for Employees Using AI?" | AI Governance |

Why each was changed:

1. Names the artefact an owner will search for and receive — the DoA matrix — instead of describing it.
2. Leads with the trigger event as it actually happens, which is how the question gets asked and searched.
3. This is the one the reviewer explicitly reframed as "owners, measures and reviews". "Decision rights" ties execution to the governance hub instead of leaving it as a standalone strategy piece; "monthly reviews" names the rhythm rather than the vague "action".
4. "Governance starting point" anchors it to the hub and keeps the scope honest — it tests interest in AI without making AI the firm's identity.

### The fifth item is a page, not a spoke

**How a Darwish & Co. Engagement Works** — the reviewer listed it as a spoke against "all
services", which is the giveaway that it is not one. It answers scope, process,
responsibility and commercial terms for the whole firm.

⚠️ In v3 it is a **standalone site page** — `/how-we-work` — linked from every service page,
the hub and the contact route. It is not part of the hub-and-spoke set and does not count
against the four spokes.

It is also the asset that closes the P004 audit failure and the invented-commercial-terms
problem, so it carries verified commercial information or it does not publish.

### Linking rules

Link where useful. **No fixed word counts, and no mandatory link in the first 150 words** —
both were removed as v1 over-engineering. Each spoke links to the hub and to its service;
the hub links to all four spokes; service pages link to the hub and relevant spokes.
Breadcrumbs carry BreadcrumbList structured data.

### Content held back

Any article naming an Egyptian or Gulf law or regulator needs qualified review and a named
reviewer before publishing (audit R-45). Replace the AI spoke entirely if no credible
delivery or review lead is available. ISO certification and board-formation articles follow
once relevance and expertise are established.

The reviewer's sharpest structural point: v1 deferred tools, research, situations and
Arabic, then built a homepage that depended on all of them. **Those dependencies are gone.**

**In the launch:** services (with verified leads), verified people pages, the hub, the four

insight hubs beyond the one.

**Unfinished features are hidden, not stubbed.** No empty sections. The agreement covers
improvements to an existing site, not an unlimited page programme.

### Homepage, launch version

Hero (agreed headline, subheadline, "Book a consultation") · the two priority situations,
with the other three as secondary rows · the six services, one line each · the hub article ·
named people · how we work · contact strip with the New Cairo office and a response-time
commitment that is actually met.

Removed from v1's homepage: readiness-check call to action, three diagnostic tools, annual
survey feature, newsletter sign-up, multiple hub cards.

## 8. URLs and migration

v1 proposed a large restructure. The reviewer rejected most of it: **improve identity and
content first; change URLs only where the information architecture warrants it.**

| v1 proposed | v3 decision |
|---|---|
| `/blog/*` → `/insights/*` | **No.** No demonstrated benefit. Keep `/blog` |
| `/partners` (consultants) → `/people`, and `/corporate-partners` → `/partners` | **Not as a swap.** Reusing `/partners` for different content needs careful migration; a new `/people` may still be worth it, separately |
| Remove `/projects` | **No.** It may discard useful evidence |
| `/training`, `/smart-solutions`: 410 now, 301 later | **No.** Keep a useful transition page until the new destination exists, then 301 each old URL to its closest replacement. 404/410 only where removed content genuinely has no replacement |
| `/services#…` fragments | Handle explicitly — done: each old anchor redirects to the page that replaced it |
